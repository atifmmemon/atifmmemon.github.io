Identifying and Quantifying Neurological Disability Via Smartphone App

“Identifying and Quantifying Neurological Disability Via Smartphone App” by Alexandra Boukhvalova, Emily Kowalczyk, Thomas Harris, Peter Kosa, Alison Wichman, Mary Sandford, Atif Memon, and Bibiana Bielekova. In ACTRIMS Forum, 2019.

Abstract

Background: Smartphone embedded sensors enable patient-autonomous measurement of neurological dysfunctions. The study hypothesized that combining data from two smartphone tests of fine finger movements would establish secondary outcomes reflecting domain-specific deficits, tested by correlating smartphone metrics with neurological exams in multiple sclerosis (MS) patients.
Methods: An Android MS test suite was developed. The study compares performance on Finger tapping and Balloon popping tests across 76 MS patients and 19 healthy volunteers.
Results: Primary smartphone outcomes effectively differentiated MS patients from healthy volunteers with power comparable to the traditional 9-hole peg test (9HPT). Secondary outcomes correlated with neurological exams to identify cerebellar dysfunction, motor fatigue, and poor eye-hand coordination or reaction time, showing low longitudinal intra-individual variance.
Conclusion: Smartphone tests offer richer, reliable measurements of distinct neurological functions in the time required for a 9HPT, indicating potential to recreate broader neurological examinations.
